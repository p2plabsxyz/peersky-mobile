# PeerSky Terms of Use

Last updated: October 6, 2026

PeerSky is a free, open source browser from P2P Labs, with PeerChat, P2PMD and
PeerTunes built in. By using it you agree to these terms. They are short on
purpose.

## PeerChat rules

PeerChat connects you straight to other people. There is no tolerance for
objectionable content or abusive users. Do not send or post:

- Sexual content involving anyone under 18. We report it to the authorities.
- Threats, harassment, bullying, or hate aimed at who someone is.
- Nudity or sexual content, in any room.
- Violence meant to shock, or anything that encourages self harm.
- Spam, scams, malware, or phishing links.
- Someone else's private information, or messages pretending to be someone else.
- Anything else that is illegal where you or the people you talk to live.

## When someone breaks them

- In groups, PeerChat filters messages for abuse, slurs and adult links, and
  refuses nude pictures before they are sent. Pictures that look explicit
  arrive hidden behind a warning.
- You can block anyone. Everything they send disappears for you, in every chat,
  and they can no longer message you directly.
- You can report anyone, or a single message, from inside the app. Reports go to
  contact@p2plabs.xyz and we read every one.
- In P2P Republic, the public room we run, we remove anyone who breaks these
  rules. Whoever creates any other room can remove people from it too.
- PeerChat has no server, so nobody, us included, can delete a message from a
  device it already reached.

## What you share

What you send in PeerChat, write in P2PMD, or publish to a drive is yours, and
you are responsible for it. It lives on your devices and on the devices of the
people you share it with. Anything published to a public `hyper://` address can
be opened by anyone who has the address, so publish only what you have the right
to share.

Opening a drive shares it too. While a drive is open, PeerSky passes the parts
of it on your phone to other people who open the same drive, as peer-to-peer
apps do. Open and keep only what you have the right to share.

## Copyright

PeerSky has no servers. We do not host, store, index or search what people
share over `hyper://`, and we do not know who publishes an address. It lives on
the devices of the people who share it and open it.

If you believe something reachable through PeerSky infringes your copyright,
email contact@p2plabs.xyz with "Copyright" in the subject, and include:

- your name and how to reach you
- the work you hold the rights to
- the exact `hyper://` address and path, or in P2P Republic the sender's name,
  the message and when it was sent
- a statement that you believe in good faith the use is not authorized by you,
  your agent or the law
- a statement that your notice is accurate and that you own the rights or act
  for the owner
- your signature; typing your full name is enough

Please do not use the public issue form on GitHub for this, since a notice
carries your name and address.

We read every notice. For a valid one:

- In P2P Republic, the public room we run, we remove whoever shared it, and
  anyone who shares it again.
- We add the address to a list built into PeerSky, so the next version of the
  app will not open it, show it or pass it on.

What we cannot do: PeerSky cannot delete anything from someone else's device or
from the network, and copies that already reached other devices stay there. A
blocked address is blocked in PeerSky only; other apps that read `hyper://` can
still open it.

If you think something was blocked by mistake, write to the same address and we
will look again. Sending a notice you know to be false can make you responsible
for the harm it does.
